# Beta 1 content audit — Everyday English

## Decision

**Approved for Beta 1: 100 units.**

The pack is intentionally **not** a list of the 100 most frequent English words. It is a compact everyday-English pack aimed roughly at an independent learner (B1/B2 territory), prioritising items that are useful to retrieve and produce: connectors, common verbs, phrasal verbs, functional chunks and contrasts that often cause mistakes.

The CEFR itself describes proficiency through communicative ability and vocabulary range rather than prescribing one official fixed vocabulary list. For Beta 1, Noma therefore uses an editorially curated pack and validates it through actual use.

## Pack profile

- 100 stable unit IDs.
- 72 single-word units.
- 28 multiword chunks.
- 20 connector / discourse / time-relation items.
- 21 phrasal or functional chunks.
- Strong contrast clusters: `since / for`, `borrow / lend`, `used to / be used to / get used to`, `although / even though / despite`.
- Everyday production contexts: work, transport, money, decisions, routines, communication and common problems.

## Audit criteria

Each unit was checked for:

1. everyday usefulness;
2. usefulness in both recognition and production;
3. excessive duplication with another unit;
4. whether the example sentence represents the intended meaning;
5. whether the Spanish prompt is reasonably specific for deterministic Beta grading;
6. whether the item adds a useful contrast or recurring-error opportunity.

## Corrections made before Beta 1

The 100 IDs remain stable. The following editorial corrections were made:

- Unit `rather` changed to **`would rather`** because the existing sentence taught preference, while the isolated word meaning was “más bien”. This removes a semantic mismatch.
- `since` clarified as **desde (punto de inicio)**.
- `for` clarified as **durante (periodo)**.
- The `through` Spanish sentence was made more explicit.
- The `within` Spanish sentence was changed to “en un plazo de una hora” to reduce ambiguity with plain `in an hour`.
- The `likely` example is now **It is likely to rain tomorrow**, matching the Spanish prompt more directly.

## Beta freeze rule

From Beta 1 onward, unit IDs 1–100 are treated as persistent learning identifiers.

- Copy edits may be made without resetting progress.
- A material change of meaning must use an explicit data migration.
- A unit must not silently be replaced with an unrelated item while keeping its ID.
- New vocabulary belongs in a future pack or an explicitly versioned pack revision.

## Tiny Reader readiness

Beta 1 contains **8 static texts**, each approximately 220–300 words with **6 closed comprehension questions**:

1. A Saturday That Didn’t Go to Plan
2. Two Days Back at the Office
3. The Package at the Wrong Door
4. A Dinner Without the Main Ingredient
5. Eight Minutes Late
6. The Class That Made Nico Go Outside
7. Repair or Replace?
8. When the Hallway Went Dark

These are sufficient for the first closed beta. More texts should be added only if real usage shows repetition is becoming a measurement problem.
